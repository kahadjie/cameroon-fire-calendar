How far can you trust this?

Limits

  • Detections, not burned area: a large fire gives several pixels, a small one may give none.
  • Clouds hide fires: a cloudy month can look quiet.
  • Sensor coverage: the main series (Suomi NPP) ends on ; provisional months (near real-time data) are never used in the answer.
How it works (3 lines)

1. Each season total is compared with the other seasons using a robust z-score: distance to the median divided by 1.4826 × the median absolute deviation; a season or month is flagged only beyond ±3.5, a threshold fixed in advance.

2. The 95% range resamples the days of each month (bootstrap), because detections cluster in time far more than a Poisson count would.

3. Sensors are compared on fire radiative power and on daytime passes at the same hour (Suomi NPP vs Aqua) near the centre of the swath, where pixels are smallest.

Words used
MODIS, VIIRS, Suomi NPP, Aqua, Terra, NOAA-20
NASA and NOAA fire sensors and satellites. MODIS (on Terra and Aqua) has 1 km pixels; VIIRS (on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21) has 375 m pixels.
FRP (fire radiative power)
The heat a fire radiates at the moment of the satellite pass, in megawatts (MW). Summed over detections it is an index of fire intensity, not an energy.
Robust z-score
How unusual a value is, measured with the median and the median absolute deviation, so that one extreme year does not hide another.
Provisional
Near real-time data that NASA may still revise.
